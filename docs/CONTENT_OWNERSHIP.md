# Content ownership and cross-references

CoreTrail explains a reusable concept in one place and links from applied examples. A page can retain a brief definition when it is needed to interpret the local example. A second full taxonomy, metric derivation or troubleshooting procedure belongs in the canonical reference.

## Choose a section, topic or chapter

Use a section for a supporting concept that fits the page's main question. Use a topic when the mechanism needs its own examples or is repeatedly referenced elsewhere. Use a chapter when several distinct topics form a coherent area. The existing modelling, architecture, storage, processing, orchestration and reliability chapters provide those groups; this pass does not add a chapter for every recurring term.

## Audit and consolidation

The pass inventoried 184 topic pages and 41 chapter overviews across the published collections. Exact-paragraph and five-word overlap scans were used to locate candidates; manual comparison of headings, examples and responsibilities determined what to merge. Similarity scores alone do not establish duplication.

Three overlapping introductions were merged into their detailed references or chapter overview. One Big Table moved from storage to modelling. Useful SQL examples were retained in their applicable SQL references. Generic ranking, probe, scaling, transaction and recovery explanations were replaced with links where another page already explains the mechanism.

Retired pages are deleted. Internal references, search inputs and navigation use the final locations. No redirect pages, route aliases or compatibility stubs are introduced.

The audit preserves short contextual definitions and distinct worked examples. It does not claim that all repeated terminology should disappear; a direct-search landing page still needs enough context to interpret its example.
